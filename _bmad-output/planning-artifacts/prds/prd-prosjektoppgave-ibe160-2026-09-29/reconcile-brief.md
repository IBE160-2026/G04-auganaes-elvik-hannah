# Brief → PRD Reconciliation

Gaps found in the PRD (`prd.md`) relative to the source brief (`brief.md`). The PRD's own "Document Purpose" section explicitly defers problem framing, competitive positioning, and long-term vision to the brief — several items below are flagged even so, because they carry decision-relevant nuance (prioritization signals, evidence caveats, emotional/positioning texture) that a reader of the PRD alone would miss, not just narrative color.

- **Landlord as explicitly named "primary user" is never stated in the PRD.** The brief's "Who This Serves" section says outright: "The landlord is the primary user — the one whose needs the product is designed around." The PRD gives landlords 2 of 3 user journeys and the largest share of added scope (portfolio dashboard, photo evidence, onboarding guide) but never states *why* — a reader of the PRD alone would not know this weighting is deliberate. (Brief §Who This Serves)

- **The "moat is fit + permission design + execution, not technology" framing is dropped entirely.** The brief is explicit and self-aware: "Honestly: the moat here isn't proprietary technology... Execution and follow-through are what would actually differentiate this in practice." This is a strategic framing that should inform where the team spends its 13 weeks of effort (e.g., polish permission/UX correctness over AI sophistication), but it appears nowhere in the PRD, including in the Open Questions about scope trade-offs. (Brief §What Makes This Different)

- **Named competitor set (Sweepy, OurHome, Tody, Flatastic) and the "points/leaderboard gamification" contrast is missing.** The brief grounds the AI assistant's value specifically against what existing chore apps do instead (gamification) and names them. The PRD's AI Assistant feature (§4.5) describes the capability but loses the "this is what makes us different from everyone else's approach" rationale — future readers won't know *why* the AI assistant exists as a differentiator rather than just a nice-to-have feature. (Brief §What Makes This Different)

- **The "must/should permission hierarchy is a structural difference, not just a feature" claim is lost.** The brief calls this out explicitly to distinguish it from a feature checklist item. The PRD treats it as FR-3/FR-5/FR-8 mechanics without ever stating that this hierarchy *is* the product's core positioning claim, not merely one feature among many. (Brief §What Makes This Different)

- **No user journey represents the single-occupant segment.** The brief names single occupancy as one of 4 served segments with an explicit distinguishing nuance: "No fairness dimension — just did I do this, and when's it due again." All three PRD user journeys (UJ-1 samboer/kollektiv, UJ-2/UJ-3 landlord/tenant) skip this segment entirely, so its simpler, non-social use case has no concrete scenario grounding it in the PRD. (Brief §Who This Serves)

- **"Trivsel" (household contentment) as a named stake is dropped.** The brief frames kollektiv stakes explicitly as "trivsel (household contentment) and fairness," not fairness alone. The PRD's JTBD and journeys (UJ-1's "Mari feels relief") gesture at contentment implicitly but never name it as a tracked value, and it's absent from Success Metrics. (Brief §The Problem)

- **Two of the brief's four supporting success signals are missing from the PRD's Success Metrics.** The brief lists 4 supporting signals: fewer overdue tasks, continued usage, "for kollektiv households: fewer chore-related conflicts reported," and "for landlords: fewer maintenance surprises or deposit disputes." The PRD's §7 "Directional signals" only carries forward 2 of these (fewer overdue tasks, continued usage) plus the review/rating signal — the segment-specific kollektiv-conflict and landlord-dispute signals are dropped outright, not just deferred. (Brief §Success Criteria)

- **The evidence caveat — "grounded in the team's own lived experience... not formal user research" — is not carried anywhere.** This matters because the PRD's JTBD and journeys state user needs with confident, validated-sounding language, while the brief is explicit that this is founder intuition, not research. A reader relying only on the PRD would not know to treat these as untested assumptions. (Brief §The Problem)

- **The "quiet resentment" emotional texture behind fairness is replaced by pure mechanism.** The brief's fairness problem is framed emotionally: "who did their part, who didn't, who's quietly resentful." The PRD's FR-11 (fairness-based targeted notification) is a clean algorithmic translation (completion-count threshold) that keeps UJ-3's "nudge, not a group scolding" framing but loses the broader emotional stakes described in the brief's problem statement. (Brief §The Problem, §Who This Serves)

- **Subscription/monetization vision (tiered individual vs. professional-landlord pricing) is absent, with no pointer back to the brief.** Unlike other deferred-to-v2 items (Hybel.no integration, payments, reviews) which the PRD explicitly cross-references to "brief's Vision," the tiered-subscription monetization model isn't mentioned anywhere in the PRD, even as a forward pointer. Given it directly implies landlords are the higher-value tier, it reinforces the "primary user" gap above. (Brief §Vision)
